A tiny world.

WorldZero is an open-source research benchmark for testing whether AI agents can discover, verify, and use hidden rules in simulated worlds. A benchmark gives different agents repeatable tests and measures what they do.

[ THE PROBLEM ]

Finding something useful once could be luck. WorldZero tests whether an agent can take an arrangement apart, rebuild it, see the effect again, and benefit from it—all within a limited energy and time budget.

This recording follows a simple, hand-written program through one test. “Food,” “energy,” and “life” are simulated resources and limits inside the experiment.

01 The agent
The orange marker is a program that chooses its next action. It can move, carry a block, eat, or wait. It sees only nearby squares.
02 The blocks
The green blocks can be moved around. In this test, a particular arrangement can change nearby food. The agent is not told which blocks matter or how to arrange them.
03 Food & energy
Food is a resource that restores energy when eaten. Richer food gives more energy. Time and actions use energy, so the agent cannot experiment forever without eating.
04 One lifetime
A lifetime is one run, lasting up to 160 simulated seconds here. Running out of energy ends it early. “Surviving” means reaching the time limit with energy left.

What counts as progress? Find a useful arrangement, test it by rebuilding it, observe its effect again, and use the result. Survival and verification are measured separately. You control the replay; the recorded program chose its own actions.

BEYOND THIS DEMO

A place to test
your own AI.

WorldZero is for researchers and developers building AI agents. Bring your own model or program, decide how it explores and remembers, and test it in worlds whose rules it has not been told.

WorldZero records what the agent does: whether it builds something useful, repeats an effect, and benefits from it. Some test worlds have no useful rule, so unsupported discovery claims count too.

This is an experimental research benchmark. The published study compares hand-written strategies; it does not yet establish how well WorldZero distinguishes the abilities of different AI models.
